A closer look at Binky

Binky is a personal information system with a home computer at its centre, private remote AI processing and evidence behind its answers. This page describes the current prototype and the direction being tested.

This is an active development project. Working paths, incomplete coverage and planned features are distinguished below. This page contains no administration credentials or private network configuration.

Where the data goes

Connected sources deliver information to the home Binky. Originals and provenance are retained while background workers prepare structured records, chunks and derived indexes. A question selects relevant evidence from those records; a private AI connection helps interpret or explain it. The saved personal corpus remains on the home computer.

Records first, indexes alongside

The authoritative corpus stores source identity, dates, supported event and transaction fields, people with roles, topics and provenance. Multiple people and relationships use linked records. Receipt time, event commencement and quoted dates remain separate. Unknown and conflicting values are retained rather than silently repaired.

Text chunks, vectors and graph relationships are derived aids to retrieval. They help locate passages and connect people, events and conversations; they do not replace original records or establish facts without evidence. Population and index coverage are still being measured.

How questions become answers

Compact classifiers and structured request plans choose the source, kind of request, filters, date window and answer shape. A personal diary or delivery question should use the personal corpus. Explicit public-information questions can take a public-information route. Action requests have separate controls.

Retrieval combines structured constraints with text and derived-index techniques where available. Answers should keep their source references and distinguish incomplete evidence. Tests include informal names, group conversations, overlapping date ranges and questions whose wording does not include “my”. Accuracy and natural wording are still being improved.

Private connections

Binky Connect uses established encrypted networking with distinct device identities and restricted service routes. The Android-to-home path and home-to-AI connection have been tested in a fresh installation. They do not require opening the customer’s router to general incoming traffic. Broader browser-only account access, recovery and additional platforms remain development work.

Diagnostics and accountability

A central diagnostics database and protected web viewer are being built for service health, structured errors, source-coverage gaps and trace references. Producers retain a local journal, retry delivery and wait for a durable acknowledgement. Stable event identities make retries safe; repeated incidents can be grouped without erasing occurrences.

The viewer uses passkey authentication. Device submission credentials have a separate, limited role. Missing heartbeats are observations, not proof of a particular root cause. Secrets and full source bodies are excluded from routine reports; sensitive traces stay at their producer. See the privacy policy for the operational-data distinction.

Installation and hardware testing

Fresh-user setup is being tested with empty stores, new permissions and normal source delivery. Classification, chunking and index work are measured on real data, newest eligible source first. Existing sources are preserved when testing new derived state.

The home appliance is intended to be modest Linux hardware, with larger AI work on Binky’s servers. We are measuring indexing load, memory, disk use, latency and recovery before promising a minimum specification. Hosted diagnostics is also sized from measured load.

What still needs work

  • More complete and continuously verified source/index coverage.
  • Clearer answers, temporal ambiguity and changing informal arrangements.
  • Secure diagnostics rollout, measured resource use and recovery testing.
  • Broader account access, onboarding, platform and historical-message support.

This is a practical development baseline, not a claim that every subsystem is finished or that any connected system is perfectly secure.
